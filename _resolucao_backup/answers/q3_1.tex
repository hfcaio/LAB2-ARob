\textbf{Discussion.}
\emph{Gyro:} the means are not zero: $\bar p=0.85$, $\bar q=-1.47$, $\bar r\approx0$ mrad/s ($0.05$, $-0.08$, $0^\circ$/s).
Since the drone is at rest and Earth-rotation ($7\times10^{-5}$~rad/s) is negligible, these are \emph{sensor biases}
(MEMS offsets, temperature dependent, only partly removed by the factory calibration); integrating $\bar q$ alone would drift
$\approx0.08^\circ$ every second, which is why a pure gyro integration is not enough (Section~4). The remaining scatter
($\sim7\times10^{-4}$~rad/s) is white-like electronic/thermal noise plus quantization of the converter, with the same level in the three axes.
\emph{Accelerometers:} at rest the sensor reads the specific force $-g\,R^\top e_3$; a perfectly levelled drone would give
$\bar a=[0,0,-9.81]$. We observe $\bar a=[0.118,-0.370,-9.916]$, i.e. $\|\bar a\|=9.92$~m/s$^2$ (+1.2\,\% scale error) and
offsets $a_y\approx-0.37$, $a_x\approx0.12$~m/s$^2$ that correspond to $2.1^\circ$ in roll and $0.7^\circ$ in pitch. This combines
a small mounting/levelling error of the table and the board with the accelerometer bias; it is a constant offset that the raw inclinometer
(3.4) inherits. The noise ($\sigma\approx0.03$~m/s$^2\approx0.2^\circ$ for $a_x,a_z$, only $0.01$~m/s$^2$ for $a_y$) is likewise
thermal/quantization noise; the raw pitch (Fig.~1) visibly toggles between discrete levels about $0.5^\circ$ ($\approx0.05$--$0.09$~m/s$^2$) apart, which is how the converter resolution
(and the sensor's internal processing) shows up. The motors being off, there is no vibration and no motion: this is the noise floor of the sensors.
